* Overview

This webpage provides supplementary materials for Section 4 (Experiments) of the
paper:

: H. Nabeshima, M. Banbara, T. Schaub, and T. Soh: The Magnificent ASP-based Nurse Scheduling System at the University of Yamanashi Hospital, submitted to ICLP 2025.

* Search Strategies

In addition to the three strategies presented in the paper (**LNPS**, **MP**,
**MP-IS**), we introduce two additional strategies, **MP-PS** and **PS**, making
a total of five search strategies for comparison.

- **LNPS** (Large Neighborhood Prioritized Search) [Sugimori et al. 2024]
  - In our LNPS implementation, if no improved solution is found within a given
    time interval $t$ ($t \in \{10,30,60\}$), the search is automatically
    restarted. Each configuration is denoted as LNPS-$t$ based on the selected
    time threshold.

- **MP** (Minimal Perturbation)
  - This search strategy minimizes modifications to the initial solution by
    introducing a penalty term into the objective function.
  - In this experiment, we set three priority levels for the objective function:
    highest, intermediate, and lowest, denoted as MP-High, MP-Mid, and MP-Low,
    respectively.

- **MP-IS** (MP with Initial-value-based Search)
  - This search strategy extends MP by incorporating an initial-value-based
    search, where retained assignments from the initial solution guide the search.
  - In the early stage of the search, to reproduce the assignments from the
    initial solution, we assign a larger-than-default value to the initial state
    of the variable selection heuristic VSIDS. We define three variations of
    this initial value: 1, 10, and 100, denoted as IS1, IS10, and IS100,
    respectively.
  - Furthermore, similar to MP, we define three variations based on priority:
    MP-ISXX-High, MP-ISXX-Mid, and MP-ISXX-Low.

- **MP-PS** (MP with Prioritized Search)
  - This search strategy extends MP by incorporating prioritized search. Similar
    to LNPS, it prioritizes the reproduction of retained assignments. As with
    MP, we define three variations: MP-PS-High, MP-PS-Mid, and MP-PS-Low.
  - While MP-IS reproduces the assignments from the initial solution only in the
    early stage of the search, MP-PS maintains this throughout all search
    phases.

- **PS** (Prioritized Search)
  - This search strategy is a variant of LNPS without automatic restarts.
  - It is included to demonstrate the impact of automatic restarts.

* Experimental Instance Settings

We evaluated our system using 50 benchmark instances simulating a typical nurse
scheduling problem (NSP) at our hospital. Each instance spans a 28-day
scheduling period with one of five nurse counts: 10, 20, 30, 40, or 50. For each
setting, 10 instances were generated with different random seeds.

The objective function adopts a four-tier priority structure:
1. *Staff preferences*
2. *Inter-shift constraints* (to avoid invalid shift sequences)
3. *Daily staffing and shift frequency constraints*
4. *Remaining soft constraints* with the lowest priority

Requested shifts—both preferred and undesired—are embedded in 10% of schedule
cells and treated as *hard constraints*. Due to this, 60% of the instances are
initially *unsatisfiable* and require relaxation. In such cases, requested
shifts are softened with higher priority than ordinary soft constraints, while
preserving the original hierarchical structure.

All instance data and the generation script are available in this repository.

* Schedule Modification Scenarios

To evaluate schedule reconstruction strategies, an initial solution for each
instance was generated within a one-hour time limit. Then, an additional 5% of
requested shifts were added, and evaluation was conducted under the following
five modification scenarios:

1. *Entire set reconstructed*: The initial solution is completely discarded, and
  a new schedule is generated from scratch. This serves as a baseline for
  comparing search strategies without relying on prior assignments.

2. *First week retained, remaining three weeks reconstructed*: Only the first 7
  days of the initial schedule are retained, and the remaining 21 days are
  reconstructed.

3. *First half retained, second half reconstructed*: The first 14 days are
  retained, and only the remaining 14 days are reconstructed.

4. *First three weeks retained, final week reconstructed*: The first 21 days are
  retained, and only the final 7 days are reconstructed.

5. *Entire set retained*: The entire initial schedule is retained.

In scenarios 2–5, the added requested shifts may also appear in the retained
portions, potentially causing constraint violations that must be resolved to
ensure feasibility.

*Note:* Among these scenarios, only scenarios 1 (*entire set reconstructed*), 3
(*first half retained, second half reconstructed*), and 5 (*entire set
retained*) are included in the results presented in the paper.
